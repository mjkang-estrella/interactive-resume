Pricing the Joint Account around how people used it

I used cohort analysis and more than 30 customer interviews to propose a 1.7 percentage-point interest-rate reduction. The change saved $4.3M annually without harming retention.

Test how much the interest rate mattered

The Joint Account paid the same 1.8% rate as regular savings accounts. I suspected that shared money management gave customers a reason to stay even at a lower rate.

I examined how money moved after an earlier reduction from 2.0% to 1.8%, comparing joint and personal accounts. Joint Account customers were less sensitive to the change. More than 30 interviews also pointed to shared financial management as a main reason for using the account.

Agree on the change and the alternatives

I presented the findings to more than 40 stakeholders across product, finance, compliance, and leadership. We agreed on a 1.7 percentage-point reduction and paths to other savings products for customers who prioritized interest income.

Result

The rate change delivered $4.3M in annual savings without harming retention or growth.
