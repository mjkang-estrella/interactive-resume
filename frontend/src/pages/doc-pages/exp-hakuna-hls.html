Choosing streaming quality we could afford to roll out

I led a phased streaming rollout at Hakuna, starting with large partner channels. Viewer engagement rose 10%, and creator satisfaction rose 20 points.

First, improve the mobile stream

Hakuna's streams were limited to 480p, and customers complained about picture quality. We raised the maximum to 720p and added adaptive bitrate switching based on device and network conditions.

We chose 720p over 1080p for this phase to balance quality and infrastructure cost. Complaints about low resolution fell by more than 60%.

Try higher-quality PC broadcasting

Professional streamers wanted to broadcast from a PC using OBS. We introduced an HLS-based option with 1080p resolution and 60 frames per second.

HLS broadcasts had 25% more viewers and 9% longer average sessions. But delay rose from about one second to seven, and infrastructure cost was about $300 per streamer per month. We kept this option limited to curated events and professional streamers.

Use WebRTC for the broader rollout

We then built PC broadcasting on the existing WebRTC protocol. It supported 720p at 30 frames per second, with lower delay and operating cost.

PC broadcasting adoption increased more than 4x. Viewers of these broadcasts watched for over 10% longer on average than viewers of mobile broadcasts.

Overall result

Across the phases, viewer engagement increased 10% and creator CSAT increased 20 points.
