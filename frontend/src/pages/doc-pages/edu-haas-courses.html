MBA Coursework

Why an MBA?

I came to Haas to bridge three threads of my career: product management, data, and purpose.

Before the MBA, I built consumer products at Toss Bank and Hyperconnect, where I learned how to ship at scale and translate data into user impact. To grow into a global product leader, I needed to strengthen two areas: strategic breadth and human depth, meaning an understanding of how technology, business models, and human behavior come together.

Haas was a natural choice because its culture of “Beyond Yourself” and its focus on responsible innovation match how I want to build products: useful, ethical, and globally scalable. Here, I’m exploring how AI and data governance shape next-generation fintech and health products, while strengthening my leadership through cross-functional teamwork and storytelling.

My courses center on AI: Agentic AI, AI Automation, AI Entrepreneurship, and The Ethics of AI.

Agentic AI (CS Department)

As a product manager, understanding AI’s capabilities and business impact is crucial, but knowing how to implement it matters just as much for building real-world solutions. That’s why I took Agentic AI, even though it’s a CS course focused on technical implementation.

Agentic AI is an emerging field that promises to transform daily life through intelligent task automation and personalization. The course covered:

  1. Core concepts: LLM foundations, reasoning, planning, agentic frameworks, and infrastructure
  2. Representative agent applications, such as code generation, robotics, web automation, and scientific discovery
  3. Limitations and risks of current LLM agents, and directions for improving them

AI Entrepreneurship

Product management is about identifying user problems and building solutions. I’ve scaled products from 1 to 10 and 10 to 100, and launched 0-to-1 products inside larger companies, like Challenge Account at Toss Bank. But I had never started a company from scratch. I took AI Entrepreneurship to learn the fundamentals and get hands-on experience building an early-stage venture.

The Ethics of AI (Philosophy Department)

I’ve long been interested in the philosophical implications of AI. As AI rapidly improves, it raises ethical questions about safety, usage, and personality. I’ve written about this on my blog (AI and Philosophy, ChatGPT Companionship), and I took this course to go deeper and debate these questions with students from other departments.

The course covered bias, privacy and surveillance, opacity, hallucinations, manipulation, and AI’s impact on education.
