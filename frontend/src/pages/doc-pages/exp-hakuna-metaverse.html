What we changed as Metaverse Live evolved

I led a team of more than ten through the roadmap, MVP, and 20+ releases of Hakuna's avatar experience. Several early ideas attracted interest without improving retention.

Start with room discovery

New users dropped out between discovering a live room, interacting, and following a streamer. We introduced Live Festa, a map where people could explore rooms.

In an A/B test, new users browsing the map spent over 20% longer in a session. Preview playback, however, reduced room entry, and next-day retention barely changed.

Move the experiment inside the room

Users spent more than 80% of their time inside live rooms. We shifted the work there, letting viewers control avatars and move around a virtual map during a stream.

Initial usage exceeded 40%, and average time spent was 5% higher than in regular rooms. The gains faded. Interviews and surveys pointed to limited room switching and too little to do on the map.

Give streamers an activity to host

We added minigames, starting with a true-or-false quiz. Streamers could customize the quiz while using a game structure supplied by the product.

This version peaked at more than 100K monthly active users. Session time doubled compared with the previous version, click-through rate rose 3.6x, and acquisition cost fell 19%.

Usage declined again as content maintenance and operating work accumulated. The games gave people more to do, but keeping that content fresh remained a problem.

Overall result

Across the project, Metaverse Live grew MAU by 10%, or 100K+ additional users. User feedback and behavior informed each change in direction.
