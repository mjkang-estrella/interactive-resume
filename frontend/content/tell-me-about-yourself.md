When I was fourteen, I wanted to become a math professor. I liked solving problems, and later studied computer science and business at KAIST.

In college, I thought I would become a consultant. Then I interned with Hyperconnect's strategy team, analyzing Azar and Hakuna from a new user's perspective. I mapped how people moved from trying the apps to using them regularly and proposed changes to those journeys.

After I presented the analysis, the team gave me access to Amplitude. It was my first time working with internal product data. I could check my assumptions against how people actually used the apps. After my final presentation, the CEO asked whether I wanted to work as a product manager. I did.

At Hyperconnect, I worked on live streaming, avatar experiences, and AI moderation. At Toss Bank, I led growth experiments and built social banking products, including Challenge Account. The work involved both customer behavior and the constraints of operating a bank.

During my MBA internship at Liner, I owned growth and monetization for its AI search API. I redesigned the evaluation and purchase journey, which tripled landing-to-paid conversion, and worked on the integrations and incentive programs that acquired its first 1,000 active API users. I also built an AI-assisted research and outbound preparation system across 1,200 accounts, contributing to an enterprise win and $400K in qualified ARR pipeline.

I'm now an MBA student at Berkeley Haas, graduating in May 2027. I take AI courses, lead workshops as the AI Club's VP of Engineering, and build my own software with AI coding agents. I'm especially interested in products that help people manage their money, health, and time.
